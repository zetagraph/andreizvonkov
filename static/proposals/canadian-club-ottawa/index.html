Understanding the Club

The new site has three jobs: get people to the next event, make 120+ years of conversations easy to explore, and make sponsorship easy to say yes to.

The Club's audience is elected officials, public servants, business leaders and civil society. They are busy, often on a phone between meetings, and expect a credible, non-partisan institution. The site should feel established and current at the same time, which is the same balance the brand refresh is aiming for.

The 125th anniversary in 2028 is a natural milestone. A site built on clean, well-structured content can carry an anniversary campaign without a rebuild.

What success looks like

  • Visitors find and register for upcoming events in two or three taps
  • Past events and recordings are searchable and easy to browse
  • Club staff update events, pages and sponsors without developer help
  • The site meets WCAG 2.1 AA and works well on any device

Recommended approach

Keep what works, rebuild what doesn't. The Club's ticketing, sponsorship payments and Guest Manager setup already run on Shopify, and staff already know it.

Replacing that would add risk, cost and retraining in the middle of a season, with no benefit to attendees. I recommend keeping Shopify as the foundation and replacing only what visitors see: a custom theme built around the refreshed brand, with structured content for events, the archive and sponsors.

This gives the Club a modern, accessible site with no disruption to event sales, no new hosting or licence fees, and a platform that can sell merchandise later without another rebuild.

Shopify themes use a templating approach very close to the content management systems I've built on for 15+ years, so the Club gets a custom theme built with the same rigour as my enterprise and government work. Staff update events, pages, sponsors and archive entries with simple structured fields, not page-building tools.

Accessibility

The RFP lists WCAG 2.1 AA as optional. I include it in the core price, because building it in from the start costs little and supports the accessibility goal in the Club's modernization plan. The site will meet WCAG 2.1 AA, in line with AODA. I hold a W3C Web Accessibility certificate.

That covers colour contrast, keyboard navigation, screen reader support, captions guidance for recordings, and accessible forms. I test with automated tools (IBM Equal Access, Lighthouse) and manually with a keyboard and screen reader before launch. Staff also get simple guidance for keeping new content accessible.

Events and archive

The site includes all the core pages in the RFP: Home, About/History, Upcoming Events, Past Events/Archive, Membership, Sponsorship and Contact. Since the Club is not a membership organization, the Membership page can cover season passes, patrons or other ways to get involved, confirmed with the Club during design.

Upcoming events show as a filterable list, which works better on phones than a calendar grid. Each event links straight to registration through the Club's existing Shopify and Guest Manager checkout. Past events, photos and recordings live in a browsable archive, organized by year and topic.

Integrations

Mailchimp signup forms connected to the Club's audience lists, and QuickBooks connected to Shopify so ticket and sponsorship sales flow into the books.

SEO and analytics

Clean page structure, metadata, redirects from current URLs, a sitemap, and Google Analytics 4 with basic event tracking for registrations.

Process and Club involvement

The Club's time is focused on a few clear decision points, timed around monthly Board meetings.

  1. Discovery. A kickoff call with the VP Operations and Communications and the Club Administrator, a review of the current site and analytics, and short conversations with the RKESTRA team and two or three regular attendees or sponsors. Output: goals, audiences and a content inventory.
  2. Structure and content plan. Site map, page layouts as wireframes, and a migration plan. The Club approves the structure and confirms which content moves.
  3. Visual design. Once the brand is approved, I design the home page and key templates. Two rounds of revisions are included.
  4. Build and migration. I build the theme on a private preview site. The current site stays live and untouched until the Club tests and approves the new one, so event promotion and registration carry on as normal. Staff can review progress at any time.
  5. Testing. Accessibility, browser and device testing, plus a check of every event and ticketing flow.
  6. Training and launch. A recorded training session and a short written guide for staff, then launch at a quiet point in the event calendar, with redirects from old URLs and a final check of every page and checkout on the live site.
  7. Support. 30 days of post-launch fixes included.

What I need from the Club: one main contact, feedback within five business days at each review, final copy and photos, and the approved brand files.

I work from London, Ontario. Reviews and Board presentations happen by video call, with in-person visits to Ottawa at key milestones when useful.
